PROJECT 15 — AUTONOMY GOVERNANCE POLICY

Service:
School-policy RAG API on the Oracle lab VM

Policy checked:
2026-08-06

The service uses action-specific autonomy instead of one global autonomous
mode. Reading disk usage, listing large directories, reading application logs,
and checking the health endpoint are ACT-tier operations because they are
read-only and have negligible blast radius. Restarting the RAG API, vacuuming
journald, clearing a package cache, or applying a reversible configuration
rollback are APPROVE-tier operations. The current platform on-call engineer
must approve each action before execution. Stopping nginx, deleting a volume,
changing firewall policy, rotating production credentials, or creating
recurring cloud cost are SUGGEST-tier decisions requiring a reviewed change
record. The agent may propose those actions but may not independently execute
them.

The tool set follows the Rule of Two. An unsupervised agent may process
untrusted logs and read narrowly scoped operational data, but it receives no
arbitrary outbound HTTP, email, or public-write tool. A task requiring
untrusted input, private access, and external communication must cross a human
gate with a destination allowlist.

Every proposal, block, approval, action, verification result, identity, and
UTC timestamp is appended to audit.log. The agent may append records but may
not rewrite previous entries. Approval tokens are valid for one action and
fifteen minutes. VERIFY rechecks the underlying metric before resolution.

Autonomous remediation may not provision more than 100 Gi in one incident or
create more than $100 of estimated monthly cost. EU AI Act Article 14 requires
effective human oversight for high-risk AI systems; this principle was
rechecked on 2026-08-06. As of that date, Annex III high-risk rules apply from
2027-12-02 and Annex I product-related rules apply from 2028-08-02. Final
accountability remains with the named human operator, not the agent.
